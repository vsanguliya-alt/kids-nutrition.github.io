18 Month Old Picky Eating: 7 Signs Parents Should Know

Picky eating is a typical and expected part of toddler development, often starting between 15 and 18 months of age . At 18 months, a child is exploring their independence and may suddenly reject foods they previously loved . However, there are important signs that distinguish normal picky eating from a more serious feeding problem.

What Is Normal Picky Eating?

Normal picky eating includes a child who:

Eats at least one food from most texture groups (e.g., crunchy, chewy, mushy) Can manage a new food on their plate and will try it with encouragement Accepts a food again if reintroduced after a break Has strong preferences but still eats a range of foods

Growth slows down after age 1, which naturally decreases appetite . Toddlers may eat well one day and very little the next—this "erratic eating" is normal .

7 Signs to Worry About Picky Eating

You should be concerned and consult your pediatrician if your 18-month-old shows any of these signs:

1. Weight Loss or Poor Growth

If your child is losing weight or not gaining weight as expected, this is a red flag .

2. Extremely Limited Diet

If your child eats fewer than 20 foods or progressively eliminates foods from their diet, this warrants attention .

3. Gagging, Choking, or Vomiting with Food

Consistent gagging or vomiting in response to eating certain foods can indicate a feeding disorder or oral-motor issue .

4. Frequent Digestive Problems

Chronic constipation, diarrhea, or stomach aches associated with eating should be evaluated .

5. Signs of Nutrient Deficiencies

Chronic fatigue, low energy, or suspected deficiencies (e.g., low iron) are warning signs .

6. Intense Mealtime Distress

If your child has severe tantrums, extreme crying, or shows intense anxiety at mealtimes, this is a concern .

7. Eating Interferes with Daily Life

When feeding difficulties heavily interfere with daily activities, family relationships, or cause significant stress, seek help .

What to Do

If you notice any of these signs, keep a food journal tracking what your child eats and their behaviors, and bring it to your pediatrician . Your doctor may refer you to a feeding specialist, occupational therapist, or registered dietitian for support .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my 18-month-old's picky eating?

Call your pediatrician if your child is losing weight, not growing as expected, gags or vomits with food, eats an extremely limited diet, or has mealtime tantrums that cause severe distress .

Q: How long does picky eating normally last?

Typical picky eating often improves by age 6. If it persists beyond this age without improvement or worsens, consult your pediatrician .

Q: What home remedies help with picky eating?

Serve new foods alongside familiar favorites, involve your child in meal preparation, limit sugary snacks between meals, and avoid pressuring or bribing them to eat. It can take 10exposures to a new food before a child accepts it .

Q: Does picky eating affect my 18-month-old's growth?

When a child eats an extremely restricted diet, they may not get enough essential nutrients like calcium, Vitamin D, or protein, which are needed for proper growth and development .

Q: Why is my 18-month-old suddenly picky?

At this age, growth slows down, reducing appetite. Toddlers also crave independence and control, making mealtime a perfect opportunity to say "no." They may also be easily distracted by exploring their environment .

Q: When should I go to the ER for feeding problems?

Go to the ER if your child has severe difficulty breathing, choking, or has a severe allergic reaction (hives, swelling, difficulty breathing). These are emergency signs. For other feeding concerns, contact your pediatrician.
