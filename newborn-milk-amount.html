How Much Milk Does a Newborn Need? A Parent&#x27;s Guide

The amount of milk a newborn needs changes rapidly during the first few weeks. For newborns, breast milk or formula provides all the hydration and nutrition they need. For a baby, breast milk is best (Source: NIH). Women who don&#x27;t have health problems should try to give their babies breast milk for at least the first six months of life (Source: NIH).

How Much Milk Does Your Newborn Need?

The amount of milk a newborn needs depends on their age, weight, and appetite. Here are general guidelines:

First Few Days

Breastfed babies: In the first few days, your baby will receive colostrum, which is rich in nutrients and antibodies. Newborns may only take about 1 to 2 ounces (30 to 60 ml) per feeding.

Formula-fed babies: In the first few days, a newborn may only take about 1 to 2 ounces (30 to 60 ml) per feeding.

First Week

Breast milk or formula: About 2 to 3 ounces (60 to 90 ml) per feeding, 8 to 12 times per day.

Total: About 16 to 24 ounces (480 to 720 ml) per day.

First Month

Breast milk or formula: About 3 to 4 ounces (90 to 120 ml) per feeding, 8 to 12 times per day.

Total: About 20 to 28 ounces (600 to 840 ml) per day.

Signs Your Newborn Is Getting Enough Milk

Steady weight gain along their growth curve.

At least 6 wet diapers per day by day 5.

Regular bowel movements (at least 3 to 4 per day in the first month).

Seeming content and satisfied after feedings.

Important Feeding Safety Tips

Avoid overfeeding: Give your baby the amount of formula or breast milk recommended (Source: NIH).

Avoid cow&#x27;s milk before age 1, since it does not have all of the nutrients that babies need and babies cannot digest it (Source: NIH).

Do not give your baby honey before 1 year of age.

Babies should not drink juice before age 1.

When to See a Doctor

If you are concerned about your baby&#x27;s milk intake, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
