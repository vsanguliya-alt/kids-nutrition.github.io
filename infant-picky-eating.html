How Much Milk Does a Baby Need? A Parent's Guide

The amount of milk a baby needs changes rapidly during the first year. For babies under 6 months, breast milk or formula provides all the hydration and nutrition they need. Breast milk contains the right balance of nutrients to help your infant grow into a strong and healthy toddler (Source: NIH). Women who don't have health problems should try to give their babies breast milk for at least the first six months of life (Source: NIH).

How Much Milk Does Your Baby Need?

The amount of milk a baby needs depends on their age, weight, and appetite. Here are general guidelines:

0 to 3 Months

Breast milk or formula: About 2 to 3 ounces (60 to 90 ml) per feeding, 8 to 12 times per day.

Total: About 16 to 24 ounces (480 to 720 ml) per day.

3 to 6 Months

Breast milk or formula: About 4 to 5 ounces (120 to 150 ml) per feeding, 6 to 8 times per day.

Total: About 24 to 32 ounces (720 to 960 ml) per day.

6 to 9 Months

Breast milk or formula: About 5 to 6 ounces (150 to 180 ml) per feeding, 5 to 6 times per day.

Total: About 24 to 32 ounces (720 to 960 ml) per day.

Solids: 2 to 3 meals per day.

9 to 12 Months

Breast milk or formula: About 5 to 6 ounces (150 to 180 ml) per feeding, 4 to 5 times per day.

Total: About 20 to 28 ounces (600 to 840 ml) per day.

Solids: 3 meals per day, plus snacks.

Signs Your Baby Is Getting Enough Milk

Steady weight gain along their growth curve.

At least 6 wet diapers per day.

Seeming content and satisfied after feedings.

When to See a Doctor

If you are concerned about your baby's milk intake, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
