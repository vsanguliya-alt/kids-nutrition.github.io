Weight Gain for a 4-Year-Old: What to Expect and When to Seek Help

At 4 years old, your child continues to grow at a steady pace, though it slows from the rapid gains of infancy and toddlerhood. Understanding healthy weight gain patterns is important for monitoring their development.

How Much Weight Should a 4-Year-Old Gain?

Between the ages of 4 and 5, a child typically gains about 4 to 5 pounds (1.8 to 2.3 kg) and grows an average of 2 to 3 inches (5 to 7.6 cm) . This represents a continued steady pace of growth during the preschool years.

How Does a 4-Year-Old Typically Weigh?

The average weight for a 4-year-old can vary. According to growth charts, an average 4-year-old boy may weigh between 32 and 40 pounds, while an 4-year-old girl may weigh between 31 and 39 pounds. These averages are general guidelines, and a wide range of weights is considered normal.

What About Growth Spurts?

Weight gain during the preschool years can happen in bursts, where a child might not gain weight for a few weeks and then have a sudden growth spurt. The most important indicator is not a single number but their consistent growth pattern over time.

Pediatricians track this using growth charts. It's the trajectory of your child's growth—the direction of the curve created by multiple measurements over time—that matters most, rather than their exact percentile. If your child is consistently growing along a curve, even if it's at a lower percentile, their growth is likely on target.

When Should I Talk to My Doctor?

Contact your pediatrician if:

Your child's weight significantly drops in percentile (e.g., crosses two major percentile lines on their growth chart).

Your child shows signs of dehydration affecting their growth (e.g., no wet diapers for 6hours).

Your child is not meeting developmental milestones alongside poor weight gain.

You have any concerns about your child's growth or appetite.
