Weight Gain for a 6-Month-Old: What to Expect

Tracking your baby&#x27;s growth is a key part of monitoring their health. For a 6-month-old, weight gain follows a specific pattern, and understanding the averages can help parents know what to expect.

How Much Weight Does a 6-Month-Old Typically Gain?

During the first 6 months, babies usually gain about 5 to 7 ounces (150 to 200 grams) per week and grow about 1/2 to 1 inch (1.5 to 2.5 cm) per month. This rapid growth is a sign that they are getting the nutrition they need. By 6 months, most babies have doubled their birth weight.

How Does a 6-Month-Old Typically Weigh?

The average weight for a 6-month-old can vary. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) growth charts, an average 6-month-old boy weighs about 17.2 pounds (7.8 kg), while an 6-month-old girl averages 15.6 pounds (7.1 kg). These are general averages, and a wide range of weights is considered normal.

What Affects Weight Gain?

A baby&#x27;s weight gain is influenced by several factors, including their diet, genetics, and overall health. Breastfed and formula-fed babies may grow at slightly different rates, but both can follow a healthy growth curve. If your baby was born prematurely, they may have a different growth pattern, which your pediatrician will monitor closely.

When Should I Talk to My Doctor?

Contact your pediatrician if:

Your baby&#x27;s weight gain slows significantly or stops.

Your baby is not meeting developmental milestones alongside poor weight gain.

You have any concerns about your baby&#x27;s feeding, growth, or appetite.

Your doctor will track your baby&#x27;s progress using a growth chart, which is the most reliable way to see if your baby is growing at a healthy rate. It&#x27;s the trajectory of your child&#x27;s growth over time that matters most, rather than their exact percentile.
