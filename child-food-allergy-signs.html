Food Allergy Signs in Children: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

An allergy is where your body reacts to something that&#x27;s normally harmless like food, pollen, dust, insect bites or animals (Source: NHS). A food allergy is an abnormal immune system reaction to certain foods (Source: NIH). It&#x27;s important for parents to recognize the signs of a food allergy, as symptoms can appear quickly or hours later .

Common Symptoms of a Food Allergy

Symptoms of a food allergy can affect any part of the body, including different parts of the body at the same time (Source: NHS). Common symptoms include:

Skin Symptoms:

Itchy skin or a raised rash (hives)

Swelling of the lips, face, and eyes (angioedema)

Redness, which can be harder to see on brown or black skin

Respiratory Symptoms:

Coughing, wheezing, or breathlessness

Noisy breathing or a hoarse voice

Sneezing or an itchy, runny, or blocked nose

Gastrointestinal Symptoms:

Diarrhea, stomach cramps, or bloating

Feeling sick or being sick

Other Symptoms:

Feeling dizzy or lightheaded

When to Seek Emergency Care

These can be signs of a serious allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) and may need immediate treatment in hospital (Source: NHS). You should seek emergency care immediately if your child has any of the following symptoms:

Sudden swelling of the lips, mouth, throat, or tongue (Source: NHS)

Breathing very fast or struggling to breathe – you may become very wheezy or feel like you&#x27;re choking or gasping for air (Source: NHS)

Throat feels tight or struggling to swallow (Source: NHS)

Skin, tongue, or lips turn blue, grey, or pale – if you have black or brown skin, this may be easier to see on the palms of your hands or soles of your feet (Source: NHS)

Food Allergy vs. Food Intolerance

A food allergy is different from having a food intolerance. A food intolerance causes symptoms such as bloating and tummy pain, usually a few hours after eating the food you&#x27;re intolerant to (Source: NHS).

What to Do If You Suspect a Food Allergy

If you think you or your child may have a food allergy, you should speak with a doctor (Source: NHS). If you suspect a severe allergic reaction, call 112 or 999 immediately.
