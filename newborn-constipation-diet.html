Newborn Constipation: A Guide for Parents

Constipation in a newborn can be distressing for both you and your baby. While it&#x27;s less common in exclusively breastfed babies, it can happen. This guide provides information on recognizing and managing constipation in your newborn.

Understanding Newborn Constipation

Constipation is not just about how often your baby poos; it&#x27;s more about the consistency of the stool. A breastfed baby may only poo once a week, but if the stool is soft and the baby is comfortable, this is not constipation.

Signs to look for:

Dry, hard, or pellet-like poo that is difficult to pass.

Straining or pain when trying to have a bowel movement.

A swollen or painful tummy.

Irritability or crying more than usual.

Dietary Changes for Constipation

For newborns, dietary changes depend on whether your baby is breastfed or formula-fed.

For Breastfed Babies

Breast milk is the perfect food for newborns and rarely causes constipation. However, if your baby is constipated:

Check your own diet: Sometimes, what you eat can affect your baby. Try increasing your own intake of fluids and high-fiber foods.

Offer more frequent feeds: Offering more frequent, shorter feeds can help stimulate your baby&#x27;s digestive system.

For Formula-Fed Babies

Constipation can sometimes be linked to the type of formula your baby is drinking.

Check the formula preparation: Ensure you are mixing the formula correctly. Too much powder can cause constipation.

Talk to your pediatrician: Your doctor may recommend switching to a different formula, such as a hydrolyzed or partially hydrolyzed formula.

Important Safety Notes

Do not give your newborn water. Do not give your baby juice or any other liquids before they start solids.

Never give laxatives to a newborn without a doctor&#x27;s prescription.

No enemas unless directed by a healthcare professional.

When to See a Doctor

Seek medical attention immediately if your newborn:

Is not gaining weight.

Has a swollen or bloated tummy.

Has vomiting (especially green).

Has blood in the stool.

Is lethargic or unwell.

Has not passed meconium (the first dark, tarry stool) within the first 48 hours of life.

If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away.
