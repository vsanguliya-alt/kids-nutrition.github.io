Starting Solid Foods at 9 Months: A Complete Guide

While the recommended time to start solid foods is around 6 months, this guide provides a helpful overview for parents who are beginning this exciting journey. At 9 months, your baby is likely well into the process of learning to eat a wide variety of foods.

Is Your Baby Ready for Solids?

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends introducing solid foods around 6 months of age, when your baby is showing signs of readiness. The sources provided confirm that infants are usually ready at about 6 months of age, but emphasize that you should always check with your health care provider for the best time for your baby to start. This is a critical step in their development. By 9 months, they should be comfortable with a range of textures and tastes.

Signs of readiness include:

Being able to sit up with minimal support.

Having good head control.

Showing interest in food by opening their mouth when food is offered.

Being able to move food from the front of the mouth to the back.

First Foods: What to Offer

Start with a single-ingredient, iron-rich food. Good first foods include:

Iron-fortified infant cereal mixed with breast milk or formula.

Pureed meat, poultry, or fish.

Pureed beans or lentils.

Pureed vegetables like sweet potatoes, carrots, or peas.

Pureed fruits like bananas, apples, or pears.

Important Safety Tips

Introduce foods one at a time. Wait 2 to 3 days before introducing a new food. This helps identify any allergic reactions.

Avoid choking hazards. Foods should be soft, smooth, and easily mashed with the gums. Cut food into small, manageable pieces.

Avoid cow's milk and honey before 12 months.

Always supervise your baby during mealtimes.

When to See a Doctor

If your baby is having trouble swallowing, especially with solid foods, or if solid foods are getting stuck in the esophagus, consult your pediatrician. These can be signs of a more serious condition.
