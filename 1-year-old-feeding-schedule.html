Feeding Schedule for a 1-Year-Old: A Guide for Parents

At 1 year old, your child is transitioning to solid foods as their main source of nutrition. This is a time of exploration—both in taste and in independence. While there&#x27;s no one-size-fits-all schedule, the following evidence-based guidance can help you establish a healthy routine.

How Much and How Often to Feed

A one-year-old&#x27;s growth rate slows down, so a decrease in appetite is normal and expected . They need about 1,000 calories per day, divided into three meals and two snacks . A general rule of thumb is to offer 1 tablespoon of each food group per year of age at each meal (for a 1-year-old, that’s 1 tablespoon each of protein, vegetables, fruit, grains, and dairy) .

Meal Frequency: Three meals and 2 to 3 snacks per day, with meals spaced about 2 to 3 hours apart .

Portion Size: The eating habits of toddlers are unpredictable. Some days they&#x27;ll eat a lot, other days very little. Don&#x27;t force them to finish their plate. Your job is to provide healthy food; their job is to decide how much to eat .

Milk Intake: At this age, the recommended daily milk intake (including dairy like yogurt and cheese) is about 360 to 480 milliliters (approximately 1½ to 2 cups) per day . After age 1, you can transition from formula to whole cow&#x27;s milk .

Sample 1-Year-Old Feeding Schedule

This is a flexible guide. Adjust meal and snack times to fit your family&#x27;s routine.

Breakfast (7:00 AM): ½ cup oatmeal with mashed banana, ½ cup whole milk. Follow with a breastfeed if nursing.

Morning Snack (10:00 AM): Sliced soft pear or yogurt.

Lunch (12:00 PM): 2tablespoons of soft-cooked chicken, rice, and steamed peas/carrots. Offer water to drink.

Afternoon Snack (3:00 PM): Small pieces of boiled egg or cheese, or whole grain crackers.

Dinner (5:30 PM): Lentils or mashed sweet potato with a small piece of whole-grain bread. Offer water to drink.

Evening (7:30 PM): Breastfeed or a light snack before bed if hungry.

Foods to Avoid and Safety Tips

Choking Hazards: A 1-year-old is still at high risk for choking because they don&#x27;t have molars for grinding food until about age 4 . Avoid whole nuts, whole grapes, hot dogs, cherry tomatoes, hard candies, popcorn, and chunks of peanut butter. Always cut food into small, non-round pieces (e.g., quarter grapes lengthwise) .

Salt and Sugar: Do not add salt or sugar to your child&#x27;s food. Avoid processed snacks high in salt and sugar .

Honey: Avoid giving honey before the age of 1 year .
