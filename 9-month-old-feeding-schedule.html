9-Month-Old Feeding Schedule: A Guide for Parents

At 9 months old, your baby is becoming more efficient at eating solids and is likely enjoying a wider variety of tastes and textures. Establishing a routine can help ensure they are getting the nutrition they need.

How Much Should a 9-Month-Old Eat?

A 9-month-old typically needs about 750 to 900 calories per day. This will be a mix of breast milk or formula (about 24 to 32 ounces / 720 to 960 ml per day) and solid foods. Your baby&#x27;s milk intake will naturally decrease as they eat more solids.

Sample Feeding Schedule

A structured routine can help your baby feel secure and help you meet their nutritional needs. Here is a sample schedule you can adapt:

Time Activity

7:00 AM Wake up

8:00 AM Breakfast

10:00 AM Morning Snack (optional)

12:00 PM Lunch

3:00 PM Afternoon Snack

5:30 PM Dinner

7:00 PM Bedtime routine begins

Important: Continue to offer breast milk or formula alongside solid meals.

What to Include in Meals and Snacks

Your 9-month-old&#x27;s diet should be rich in nutrients from a variety of food groups.

Fruits and Vegetables: Offer a colorful variety, cooked until soft or pureed.

Protein: Eggs, poultry, fish, beans, and lentils.

Healthy fats: Avocado, nut butters (thinly spread), and full-fat yogurt.

Complex carbohydrates: Whole grain cereals, oatmeal, and soft-cooked pasta.

Common Feeding Challenges

Picky Eating: Around this age, babies can become pickier about food. Continue to offer a wide variety of foods, and do not force your baby to eat.

Refusing Food: Babies may refuse food for many reasons—they are full, teething, or tired. Follow their lead and trust their appetite.

Choking Hazards: Always offer foods that are soft, small, and easy to chew. Cut foods into small, manageable pieces to avoid choking hazards.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your baby&#x27;s eating habits, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
