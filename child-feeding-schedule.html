Child Feeding Schedule: A Guide for Parents

Establishing a consistent feeding schedule is an important part of caring for your child. While the exact schedule will vary based on your child&#x27;s age and needs, understanding the basics can help you create a routine that works for your family.

Feeding Schedule by Age

6 to 9 Months

Breast milk or formula: About 24 to 32 ounces (720 to 960 ml) per day.

Solids: 2 to 3 meals per day.

Snacks: Optional.

Water: Offer a few sips of water with meals.

9 to 12 Months

Breast milk or formula: About 20 to 28 ounces (600 to 840 ml) per day.

Solids: 3 meals per day.

Snacks: 2 snacks per day.

Water: Offer water with meals.

12 to 24 Months

Breast milk or formula: About 16 to 24 ounces (480 to 720 ml) per day.

Solids: 3 meals per day.

Snacks: 2 snacks per day.

Water: Offer water with meals and between meals.

2 to 5 Years

Milk: About 2 to 2.5 cups (480 to 600 ml) of milk per day.

Solids: 3 meals per day.

Snacks: 2 snacks per day.

Water: Offer water with meals and between meals.

What to Include in Meals and Snacks

A healthy diet should include a variety of foods from all food groups:

Fruits and vegetables: Make half of what is on your child&#x27;s plate fruits and vegetables.

Protein: Choose healthy sources of protein, such as lean meat, poultry, fish, eggs, beans, and nuts.

Whole grains: Choose whole-wheat bread, oatmeal, and brown rice.

Dairy: Offer milk, yogurt, and cheese.

When to See a Doctor

If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s eating habits, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. Well-child visits are important to your child&#x27;s health. If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider.
