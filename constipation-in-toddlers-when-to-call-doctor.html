Constipation in Toddlers: When to Call the Doctor

Constipation in toddlers is common, affecting nearly 1 in 20 pediatric visits, and usually resolves with home care . However, certain symptoms signal when medical attention is necessary.

What Defines Constipation?

Constipation means having fewer than two bowel movements a week, with stools that are hard, dry, lumpy, or painful to pass . Toddlers may show signs like standing on tiptoes, clenching buttocks, or doing "dancelike" movements to avoid a painful bowel movement .

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your pediatrician if constipation lasts more than 2 weeks or doesn't improve with home treatment .

Seek immediate medical attention for any of these "red flag" symptoms :

Blood in the stool or bleeding from the rectum

Vomiting (especially green or bilious vomiting)

Severe abdominal pain, distension (swelling), or bloating

Weight loss or lack of appetite

Fever with constipation

Special Considerations for Infants

Any infant under 2 months with constipation needs a doctor's evaluation. For non-breastfed infants, contact a provider immediately if they go 3 days without a stool and are vomiting or irritable .

When Constipation May Indicate a Serious Condition

If constipation is accompanied by abnormal neurological signs, leg weakness, or anal/sacral abnormalities, urgent referral is needed as these may indicate an underlying organic cause .
