Sugar Limits for Children: What New York Parents Should Know

Limiting added sugar is a key focus of public health efforts in New York City, especially for young children. With new local regulations and a clear understanding of the risks, parents have more support than ever to help their children develop healthy habits.

The Official Sugar Limit

For children, the recommended daily limit for added sugar is less than 25 grams (about 6 teaspoons) per day, according to the American Heart Association . In New York City, sugary drink consumption among young children is a known concern: in 2015, children ages 0consumed an average of 0.53 sugary drinks per day .

Local Regulations in New York

New York City and State have implemented several measures to help families reduce sugar intake:

NYC Childcare Regulations: City-licensed childcare centers are required to serve no beverages with added sweeteners. Juice is limited to 6 ounces per day for children over 8 months old .

NYC Food Standards 2025: For meals served by city agencies, yogurt must contain no more than 7g added sugar per 4 oz (and no more than 3g per 4 oz for children ages 2). Cereal is limited to 6g added sugar per serving .

NYC Sweet Truth Act: Chain restaurants must display warning labels on menu items containing over 50 grams of added sugar .

NYS School Nutrition Standards: School cereals and yogurts must also meet strict sugar limits .

How to Reduce Your Child's Sugar Intake

Check Labels for Added Sugar: Sugar hides in many processed foods like pasta sauces, soups, yogurts, and even breads. Look for words like high-fructose corn syrup, evaporated cane juice, and rice syrup .

Drink Water or Milk: Replace sugary drinks (soda, juice, sports drinks) with water and plain milk . Note that babies should not drink juice before age 1.

Choose Whole Fruits Over Juice: Fruit juice destroys fiber and turns a healthy snack into a sugary drink. Instead, offer fresh fruit like berries or apples .

Limit Fast Food and Packaged Snacks: Opt for whole foods—fruit, vegetables, nuts, eggs—instead of processed snacks .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my child's sugar intake?

Talk to your pediatrician if you are concerned about your child's eating habits, especially if they are consistently consuming large amounts of sugary foods and drinks, showing signs of unhealthy weight gain, or if you have concerns about their dental health.

Q: Is fruit juice safe for my toddler?

For children under 1, juice should not be given. For children over 1, limit 100% fruit juice to no more than 4ounces per day, and always serve it in a cup, not a bottle .

Q: What snacks are healthy for a 3-year-old?

Offer whole foods like apple slices with peanut butter, plain yogurt with berries, or hard-boiled eggs. Check labels and avoid products with added sugars .

Q: How can I tell if a food has added sugar?

Read the nutrition label. Look for "added sugars" in the nutrition facts panel and scan the ingredients list for words like syrup, honey, or any ingredient ending in "-ose" (like sucrose, fructose, dextrose) .

Q: Are artificial sweeteners safe for children?

Artificial sweeteners are generally not recommended for young children. Focus on reducing added sugar by choosing naturally sweet whole foods instead .

Q: When should I go to the ER for a sugar-related issue?

Go to the ER immediately if your child has severe difficulty breathing, choking, or a severe allergic reaction to a food. These are emergency signs. For dietary concerns, contact your pediatrician.
