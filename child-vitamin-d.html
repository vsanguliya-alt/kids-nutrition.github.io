Vitamin D for Your Child: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Vitamin D is an essential nutrient that helps your child&#x27;s body absorb calcium and phosphorus, which are critical for building strong, healthy bones . A lack of vitamin D can lead to rickets, a condition that causes bone pain, muscle weakness, delayed growth, and skeletal deformities such as bowed legs or thickened wrists and ankles .

Daily Vitamin D Recommendations

The recommended daily intake of vitamin D for children varies by age:

Age Group Daily Recommended Vitamin D

Under 12 months 400 IU (10 mcg) 1 to 3 years 600 IU (15 mcg) In the United Kingdom, health authorities recommend that all children under 5 years old take a daily supplement of 10 micrograms (400 IU) of vitamin D .

Why Supplements Are Often Recommended

Several factors make it difficult for children to get enough vitamin D from diet and sun exposure alone:

Limited Food Sources: Vitamin D is not naturally abundant in many foods. It is found in fatty fish (like salmon), eggs, and some fortified foods like milk, yogurt, and cereals .

Insufficient Sun Exposure: Factors like sunscreen use, skin pigmentation, season, and geography affect how much vitamin D the body can produce from sunlight . Children often spend more time indoors, further limiting natural production.

Because of this, many health organizations recommend vitamin D supplements for all young children.

Preventing Rickets

Rickets is a serious but preventable condition. It can cause permanent bone deformities and delays in growth and motor skills if not treated . Ensuring your child meets their daily vitamin D requirement is the most effective way to prevent rickets, particularly in infants and toddlers .

How to Ensure Your Child Gets Enough Vitamin D

Dietary Sources: Incorporate vitamin-D rich foods into your child&#x27;s meals. Examples include:

Fatty fish like salmon and tuna .

Eggs (the yolk) .

Vitamin D-fortified whole cow&#x27;s milk (for children over 12 months) .

Fortified yogurt, cereals, and 100% juices .

Supplements: Vitamin D supplements are an effective and common way to meet daily needs. They are available as drops, liquids, or chewable tablets .

When to See a Doctor

If you are concerned about your child&#x27;s vitamin D intake, growth, or if you suspect signs of a deficiency (such as bone pain, muscle weakness, or delayed milestones), speak with your pediatrician . They can provide personalized guidance and, if necessary, recommend a suitable supplement and dose . Children who are asymptomatic but at risk may not require a blood test .
