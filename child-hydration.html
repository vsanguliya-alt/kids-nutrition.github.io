Child Hydration: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Keeping your child well-hydrated is essential for their health. Water helps regulate body temperature, aids digestion, protects organs and joints, and helps with blood pressure and maintaining focus. Understanding how much fluid they need and what to offer is key.

Daily Fluid Intake

The amount of fluid a child needs depends on their age, weight, and activity level. Here are general guidelines:

1 to 3 years: About 4 cups (1 liter) of fluid per day.

4 to 8 years: About 5 cups (1.2 liters) of fluid per day.

This total includes water, milk, and other beverages. Fluid needs can increase with activity, hot weather, or during illness (fever, vomiting, diarrhea). Ensuring your child has regular access to water throughout the day, especially during and after physical activity, is the best way to meet their needs.

What to Offer and What to Avoid

Offer:

Water: The healthiest and cheapest choice for hydration.

Milk: Provides essential nutrients like calcium.

Avoid:

Sugary drinks: Children should avoid sugary fizzy drinks, squash, and fruit juice drinks completely. The added sugar can lead to being overweight and damage teeth.

Sports drinks: While they replace salts, they can contain large amounts of sugar, which can worsen diarrhea if your child is sick.

Hydration During Illness

When your child is sick with vomiting or diarrhea, preventing dehydration is crucial.

Continue offering fluids: The goal should be at least 1 ounce (30ml) per hour.

Oral rehydration solutions (ORS): If your child cannot keep fluids down, consider a commercial rehydration solution that contains sugars and salts.

Small, frequent amounts: For a child over 1 year old, give 1/2 to 1 ounce (1 to 2 tablespoons or 15 to 30 mL) every 20 minutes for a few hours.

When to See a Doctor

Call your child&#x27;s doctor right away if your child shows any signs of dehydration, such as:

Urinating less frequently (for children, fewer than 3wet diapers or trips to the toilet in 24 hours).

Dry tongue and inside of mouth, dry eyes, and fewer tears when crying.

Excessive sleepiness or fussiness.

Wrinkled skin.

Is too sick to drink or becomes lethargic.
