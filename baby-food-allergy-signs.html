Food Allergy Signs in Babies: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

An allergy is where your body reacts to something that&#x27;s normally harmless like food, pollen, dust, insect bites or animals (Source: NHS). A food allergy is caused by your immune system overreacting to certain types of food (Source: NHS). When you introduce solid foods to your baby, it&#x27;s important to be aware of the signs of a food allergy.

Common Symptoms of a Food Allergy

Symptoms of a food allergy can affect any part of the body, including different parts of the body at the same time (Source: NHS). They can appear quickly after eating a food, or in some cases, days later (Source: NHS).

Skin Symptoms:

Itchy skin or a raised rash (hives)

Swelling of the lips, face, and eyes (angioedema)

Respiratory Symptoms:

Coughing, wheezing, breathlessness, noisy breathing, or a hoarse voice

Sneezing or an itchy, runny, or blocked nose

Gastrointestinal Symptoms:

Feeling sick or being sick

Diarrhea

Tummy pain

Other Symptoms:

Feeling dizzy or lightheaded

When to Seek Emergency Care

These can be signs of a serious allergic reaction and may need immediate treatment in hospital (Source: NHS). You should seek emergency care immediately if your child has any of the following symptoms:

Severe difficulty breathing – gasping, choking, or not able to get words out (babies may make grunting noises or their stomach may suck under their ribcage) (Source: NHS)

Swelling of the lips, face, and eyes (Source: NHS)

Blue, grey, pale, or blotchy skin, lips, or tongue – on brown or black skin this may be easier to see on the palms of the hands or soles of the feet (Source: NHS)

Confusion or not responding as usual (Source: NHS)

Common Food Allergens

The most common allergens include cow&#x27;s milk, eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, soy, wheat, fish, and shellfish.

Introducing Allergenic Foods

If you introduce one new food at a time, you will be able to identify any foods that cause allergies in your baby (Source: NIH). When babies can eat foods that contain peanuts depends on their risk of food allergies. Most babies can have peanut products when they are about 6 months of age. Babies who have mild to moderate eczema have a higher risk of food allergies (Source: NIH).

Food Allergy vs. Food Intolerance

A food allergy is different from having a food intolerance, which causes symptoms such as bloating and tummy pain, usually a few hours after eating the food you&#x27;re intolerant to (Source: NHS).

What to Do If You Suspect a Food Allergy

If you think you or your child may have a food allergy, you should speak with a doctor (Source: NHS). If you suspect a severe allergic reaction, call 112 or 999 immediately.
