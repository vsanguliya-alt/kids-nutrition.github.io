Sugar Limit for Your Child: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

The early years are a critical time for establishing healthy eating habits. Limiting added sugar is an important part of this, as it supports your child&#x27;s growth, development, and long-term health.

The Recommendation

The American Heart Association (AHA) recommends that children between 2 and 18 years of age consume less than 25 grams (about 6 teaspoons) of added sugar per day. For children under 2, the recommendation is no added sugars at all.

Why Sugar Should Be Limited

Nutritional Displacement: Foods with added sugar often offer empty calories and can displace healthier, nutrient-dense foods like fruits, vegetables, and proteins.

Developing Taste Preferences: Introducing sweet foods early can shape a child&#x27;s long-term taste preferences, making them more likely to prefer sweet foods later in life.

Dental Health: Sugar promotes the growth of bacteria that cause tooth decay.

Health Risks: Excessive sugar intake is linked to an increased risk of childhood obesity, type 2 diabetes, and other health issues.

Sources of Added Sugar to Watch Out For

Sweetened beverages (juice, soda, fruit drinks, sports drinks)

Sweetened yogurts

Processed snacks and cereals

Baked goods and desserts

What to Offer Instead

Water or milk instead of sugary fruit drinks and sodas (Source: NIH).

Fruit: Offer whole fruits, which provide natural sweetness along with fiber and nutrients.

Healthy snacks: Offer foods like nuts, seeds, yogurt, and vegetables.

Limit fast food and junk food (Source: NIH).

Tips for Reducing Sugar

Read labels: Look for added sugars on food labels.

Offer water: Keep water available throughout the day.

Make healthy foods accessible: Keep fruits and vegetables within easy reach for snacks.

When to See a Doctor

If you have any concerns about your child&#x27;s diet or growth, talk to your pediatrician. They can offer personalized guidance and support.
