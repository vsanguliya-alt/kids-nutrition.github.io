Iron-Rich Foods for Your Infant: A Complete Guide

Iron is a vital mineral that supports your baby's growth and brain development. Your baby's body uses iron to make hemoglobin, a protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body. It is also important for healthy muscles, bone marrow, and organ function, and is needed to make some hormones (Source: NIH).

How Much Iron Does Your Baby Need?

Babies are born with iron stores that last about six months. After this, they need to get iron from their diet to prevent deficiency, which can affect their ability to learn and pay attention. The recommended daily iron intake is 11 mg for babies aged 7 to 12 months.

The Best Iron-Rich Foods

Iron in food comes in two forms: heme and non-heme. Heme iron, found in animal products, is more easily absorbed by the body than non-heme iron from plants (Source: NIH).

Heme Iron Sources (Animal-Based)

Lean meat, seafood, and poultry (Source: NIH).

Eggs: A great source of iron and protein.

Fish: Salmon, trout, and tuna.

Non-Heme Iron Sources (Plant-Based)

Iron-fortified breakfast cereals and breads (Source: NIH).

White beans, lentils, spinach, kidney beans, and peas (Source: NIH).

Nuts and some dried fruits, such as raisins (Source: NIH).

How to Boost Iron Absorption

The body doesn't absorb non-heme iron as easily, but you can help by pairing it with vitamin C. This significantly increases how much iron your baby takes in.

Great vitamin C pairings include:

Fruits: Oranges, strawberries, kiwi, and mango.

Vegetables: Broccoli, bell peppers, and tomatoes.

What to Avoid

Cow's milk before 12 months: It contains very little iron and can actually make it harder for the body to absorb iron from other foods. It also fills your baby's small stomach, reducing their appetite for iron-rich foods.

Honey before 12 months: This is a safety risk due to infant botulism, not iron absorption.

When to See a Doctor

If you are concerned that your baby might not be getting enough iron, or if you have any questions about their nutrition and growth, speak with your pediatrician. They can provide guidance and, if necessary, recommend a suitable supplement and dose.
