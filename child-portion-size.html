Child Portion Sizes: A Guide for Parents

Feeding a child can be confusing, especially when it comes to portion sizes. Unlike older children and adults, a child's stomach is smaller, and their needs change as they grow. This guide offers a general framework for understanding appropriate portion sizes for your child.

Why Portion Sizes Are Different for Children

A child's stomach is about the size of their fist. This means they cannot eat large volumes of food in one sitting. Instead of three large meals, they often need to eat smaller amounts more frequently—three meals and two to three snacks a day.

General Guidelines for Portion Sizes

There is no one-size-fits-all approach, but these general guidelines can be a helpful starting point for a child.

Grains (bread, cereal, rice, pasta): ½ slice of bread, or ½ cup (about 120 ml) of cooked pasta or rice.

Vegetables: 2 to 3 tablespoons (about 30ml) of cooked vegetables.

Fruits: ½ cup (about 120 ml) of fresh, canned, or cooked fruit.

Protein (meat, poultry, fish, beans): 2 to 3 tablespoons (about 30ml) of cooked meat, or ½ cup (about 120 ml) of cooked beans.

Dairy: ½ cup (about 120 ml) of milk or yogurt, or 1 ounce (about 30 grams) of cheese.

How to Know If Your Child Is Eating Enough

Rather than focusing on the exact measurement, it's often more useful to pay attention to your child's hunger and fullness cues. A child who is eating enough will:

Have steady weight gain and follow their own growth curve on the pediatrician's growth charts.

Have normal energy levels and be generally content.

Produce regular wet diapers.

What to Do If Your Child Refuses Food

It is common for children to be picky or reject foods. This is a normal part of their development as they assert their independence. Avoid pressuring or forcing them to eat, as this can create negative associations with food. Instead:

Offer a variety of healthy foods at each meal.

Be a role model by eating the same foods.

Trust them to eat what they need over the course of a week, rather than at a single meal.

When to See a Doctor

If you are concerned that your child is not eating enough, is not gaining weight appropriately, or is missing developmental milestones, speak with your pediatrician. They can offer personalized advice and reassurance.
