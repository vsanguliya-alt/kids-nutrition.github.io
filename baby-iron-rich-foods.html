Iron-Rich Foods for Your Baby: A Complete Guide

Iron is a vital mineral that supports your baby's growth, brain development, and immune function . Babies are born with iron stores that last about six months. After this, they need to get iron from their diet to prevent deficiency, which can affect their ability to learn and pay attention .

How Much Iron Does Your Baby Need?

The recommended daily iron intake is 11 mg for babies aged 7 to 12 months . While this is the target, the AAP has also updated screening recommendations. For primarily breastfed infants, a blood test to check for anemia is now recommended between 9 and 12 months of age .

The Best Iron-Rich Foods

Iron in food comes in two forms: heme and non-heme. Heme iron, found in animal products, is more easily absorbed by the body than non-heme iron from plants .

Heme Iron Sources (Animal-Based)

Red meat: Beef, lamb, pork, and goat are excellent sources .

Poultry: Chicken and turkey .

Seafood: Fish like salmon, trout, and mackerel are good choices. Do not give liver to babies under 12 months as it contains too much vitamin A .

Eggs: Well-cooked eggs are a great source of iron .

Non-Heme Iron Sources (Plant-Based)

Iron-fortified infant cereals: These are a staple for this age group .

Legumes: Cooked and mashed lentils, chickpeas, and beans .

Tofu: A versatile source of iron .

Dark green leafy vegetables: Spinach, kale, and broccoli (well-cooked) .

Dried fruits: Puréed or chopped raisins, prunes, and apricots .

How to Boost Iron Absorption

The body doesn't absorb non-heme iron as easily, but you can help by pairing it with vitamin C. This significantly increases how much iron your baby takes in .

Great vitamin C pairings include:

Fruits: Strawberries, kiwi, mango, papaya, oranges, and plums .

Vegetables: Broccoli, sweet potatoes, bell peppers, and tomatoes .

For example, you can mix mashed berries into iron-fortified cereal or serve pureed broccoli alongside a lentil puree .

What to Avoid

Cow's milk before 12 months: It contains very little iron and can actually make it harder for the body to absorb iron from other foods . It also fills your baby's small stomach, reducing their appetite for iron-rich foods .

Honey before 12 months: This is a safety risk due to infant botulism, not iron absorption.

Creative Ways to Serve Iron-Rich Foods

If your baby is losing interest in purees, try these ideas:

Fortified cereal pancakes: Replace half the flour in your pancake mix with dry infant cereal .

Mix cereal into yogurt or fruit puree for an extra iron boost .

Offer soft-cooked finger foods like strips of meat, tofu, or well-cooked vegetable sticks.
