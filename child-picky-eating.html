Child Picky Eating: What Parents Can Do

Picky eating is a common phase for many children, especially toddlers and preschoolers. It's a normal part of their development as they assert their independence. However, it can be a source of stress for parents who worry about their child's nutrition and growth.

Why Do Children Become Picky Eaters?

There are several reasons why a child might become picky:

Developmental Stage: As children grow, they become more aware of their environment and may be easily distracted during mealtimes.

Independence: They are learning to assert their independence and may refuse food simply because they can.

Neophobia (Fear of New Things): Many children are naturally cautious about trying new foods.

Changes in Appetite: Growth spurts often cause a temporary increase in appetite, followed by a decrease.

Strategies for Handling Picky Eating

Here are some evidence-based strategies to navigate this phase:

1. Keep Offering a Variety of Foods

Continue to offer a wide range of healthy foods, even if your child has rejected them before. It can take up to 10 to 15 exposures for a child to accept a new food. Don't force the issue, but keep offering.

2. Make Mealtimes Positive

Avoid pressure and power struggles. Let your child explore food at their own pace. It's okay if they don't eat much; mealtimes are about learning and socializing too.

3. Involve Your Child

Involve your child in the meal planning and preparation process. This can increase their interest in trying new foods.

4. Offer Choices

Give your child a sense of control by letting them choose between two healthy options. For example, "Would you like carrots or peas with your dinner?"

5. Be a Role Model

Children learn by watching. Eat a variety of healthy foods yourself and show your child that you enjoy them.

6. Stick to a Routine

A regular meal and snack schedule can help your child feel secure and regulate their appetite.

7. Create a Healthy Plate

Make half of what is on your child's plate fruits and vegetables. Choose healthy sources of protein, such as lean meat, nuts, and eggs. Serve whole-grain breads and cereals because they are high in fiber (Source: NIH).

When to See a Doctor

While picky eating is usually normal, you should consult your pediatrician if your child:

Is not gaining weight or is losing weight.

Is refusing all foods and drinks.

Has signs of a medical issue, such as persistent vomiting, diarrhea, or severe constipation.

Is showing signs of a developmental delay.

If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider right away.
