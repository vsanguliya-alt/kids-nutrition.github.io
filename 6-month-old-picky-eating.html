When Should You Worry About a 6-Month-Old's Picky Eating?

At 6 months old, babies are just starting to explore solid foods. Their eating behaviors are not typically described as "picky eating," which is a term more commonly used for toddlers and older children. Instead, a baby's response to new foods is often about learning and sensory exploration.

What Is Normal at 6 Months?

It is completely normal for a 6-month-old to be hesitant about new tastes and textures. This is a new experience for them, and it can take time to adjust. Here's what normal looks like:

Making faces: Pushing food out, spitting, or making funny expressions are normal reactions to new flavors.

Refusing at first: Many babies need to try a new food 10–15 times before they accept it. It's not pickiness, it's learning.

Eating very small amounts: At 6 months, solid foods are complementary to breast milk or formula. It's normal for babies to eat just a spoonful or two.

Showing interest: Reaching for food, opening their mouth, or watching you eat are signs of readiness.

When Should You Worry? (Red Flags)

While hesitation is normal, you should consult your pediatrician if your 6-month-old:

Refuses all solid foods after consistent attempts over several weeks.

Is not gaining weight or is losing weight.

Has difficulty swallowing or frequently chokes, gags, or vomits when eating.

Shows signs of dehydration (fewer wet diapers, sunken eyes, no tears).

Has a sudden and significant change in appetite that lasts more than a few days, especially if accompanied by other symptoms like fever or diarrhea.

Is not meeting developmental milestones related to feeding, such as sitting with minimal support or showing interest in food.

What to Do

Be patient: Keep offering a variety of foods. It can take many exposures for a baby to accept a new food.

Offer a safe environment: Sit or hold your baby upright during feeding to stop them swallowing air (Source: NHS | Colic – Colic).

Make it positive: Avoid pressuring or forcing your baby to eat. Let them explore food at their own pace.

Follow safe sleep guidelines: "Tummy time" is for when babies are awake and someone is watching (Source: NIH | Sudden Infant Death Syndrome: MedlinePlus).

Talk to your pediatrician: If you have any concerns about your baby's eating habits, growth, or development, discuss them with your health care provider.

The Bottom Line

Picky eating is not a typical concern for a 6-month-old. At this age, it's normal to be hesitant about new foods. If your baby is refusing all solids, not gaining weight, or showing signs of illness, contact your pediatrician. Early intervention can help address feeding challenges and ensure your baby gets the nutrition they need for healthy growth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my 6-month-old's eating habits?

Call your pediatrician if your baby refuses all solid foods, is not gaining weight, has difficulty swallowing, or shows signs of illness (fever, diarrhea, or dehydration). If you are worried, talk to your pediatrician.

Q: How long does it take for a 6-month-old to accept new foods?

It can take 10 to 15 exposures or more for a baby to accept a new food. Be patient and persistent.

Q: What home remedies help with feeding difficulties?

Offer a variety of foods, make mealtime positive, and avoid pressuring your baby to eat. Let them explore food at their own pace. Sit or hold your baby upright during feeding to stop them swallowing air (Source: NHS | Colic – Colic).

Q: Is it normal for a 6-month-old to refuse solids?

Yes, it is common for babies to refuse new foods at first. However, if they are refusing all solids and not gaining weight, it is a concern. Talk to your pediatrician.

Q: Does teething affect a 6-month-old's eating habits?

Teething can cause discomfort and lead to temporary changes in eating habits, but it should not last more than a few days. If your baby is otherwise healthy and continues to gain weight, it is usually not a cause for worry.

Q: When should I go to the ER for a 6-month-old with feeding problems?

Go to the ER if your baby has severe difficulty breathing, choking, or has a severe allergic reaction (hives, swelling, difficulty breathing). These are emergency signs. For other concerns, contact your pediatrician.
