Toddler Milk Intake in Children 0: Complete Parent Guide

Milk is a cornerstone of a toddler's diet, providing essential nutrients like calcium, vitamin D, and protein. However, the transition from formula or breast milk to cow's milk—and the right amount—can be confusing. This guide covers evidence-based recommendations to help you navigate your child's milk intake from birth to age 5.

The First Year: Breast Milk or Formula Only

During the first 12 months of life, cow's milk is not recommended as a main drink. It does not contain the right balance of nutrients that babies need and can be hard for them to digest . Some of the nutrients in breast milk also help protect your infant against some common childhood illnesses and infections .

Key points for the first year:

Breast milk or formula should be the primary source of nutrition

Cow's milk should not be introduced before 12 months of age Babies should not drink juice before age 1, as it contains a lot of sugar and can displace milk intake 12 to 24 Months: Introducing Whole Milk

Once your child turns one, you can introduce pasteurized whole cow's milk
