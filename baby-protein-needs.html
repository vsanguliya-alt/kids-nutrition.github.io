Protein Needs for Your Baby: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Protein is an essential nutrient that plays a vital role in your baby&#x27;s growth and development. Ensuring your baby gets enough protein supports everything from building muscles to maintaining a healthy immune system.

Why Protein Is Important

Proteins are the building blocks of the body. They help to build and repair tissues, produce enzymes and hormones, and support a healthy immune system. For a growing baby, adequate protein intake is critical for healthy development. Making healthy food choices every day will help you give your baby what they need to develop (Source: NIH).

How Much Protein Does a Baby Need?

The amount of protein a baby needs changes as they grow.

0 to 6 months: Babies get all the protein they need from breast milk or formula. Breast milk is best for babies (Source: NIH).

6 to 12 months: As babies start solids, they need about 11 grams of protein per day.

Here is what 11 grams of protein looks like in everyday foods:

1 egg

½ cup (about 120 ml) of whole milk yogurt

2 ounces (about 60 grams) of chicken, turkey, or fish

½ cup (about 120 ml) of cooked beans or lentils

1 tablespoon of peanut butter

Best Sources of Protein for Your Baby

Babies benefit from a variety of protein sources. These include both animal and plant-based options.

Animal sources: Eggs, poultry, fish, yogurt, and cheese.

Plant-based sources: Cooked beans, lentils, and tofu.

Signs Your Baby Might Not Be Getting Enough Protein

Protein-energy undernutrition happens when your body doesn&#x27;t get enough protein or calories from food (Source: NIH). Signs to watch for include:

Poor growth or not gaining weight as expected.

Lack of energy or seeming tired more often than usual.

Frequent infections or a weakened immune system.

Tips for Including Protein in Your Baby&#x27;s Diet

Start early: Offer a source of protein at every meal, such as eggs, yogurt, or beans.

Get creative: Babies may be more willing to eat protein if it&#x27;s presented in fun shapes or mixed with foods they love (like adding mashed beans to pasta sauce).

Let them self-feed: Offer small, soft pieces of protein-rich foods that they can easily pick up, which helps build confidence and motor skills.

When to See a Doctor

If you are worried that your baby isn&#x27;t getting enough protein or isn&#x27;t growing at a healthy rate, speak with your pediatrician. A healthcare professional can assess your baby&#x27;s growth and provide personalized guidance on their nutritional needs. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
