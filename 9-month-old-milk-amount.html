How Much Milk Does a 9-Month-Old Need?

At 9 months old, your baby is becoming more skilled at eating solids, and their milk intake will naturally start to decrease. Understanding how much milk they need is important for their growth and development.

The Recommended Amount

A 9-month-old typically needs about 24 to 32 ounces (720 to 960 ml) of breast milk or formula per day. This is often spread out over 3 to 5 feedings. The exact amount can vary based on your baby's size, appetite, and how much solid food they are eating.

Balancing Milk and Solids

As your baby eats more solid foods, their milk intake will gradually decrease. At 9 months, solid foods should provide a significant portion of their nutrition. However, breast milk or formula should still be the main source of nutrition until age 1.

Signs your baby is getting enough milk:

Steady weight gain along their growth curve.

Producing at least 6 wet diapers a day.

Seeming content and satisfied after feedings.

Tips for Managing Milk Intake

Offer milk before solids: At this age, milk is still a primary source of nutrients. Offer milk first, then solids about an hour later.

Follow your baby's cues: Your baby will let you know when they are full. Don't force them to finish a bottle or breastfeed if they are not hungry.

Avoid overfeeding: Overfeeding can cause discomfort and spit-up. Pay attention to your baby's signals.

Use a sippy cup: Introduce a sippy cup with water during meals. This helps your baby transition away from the bottle and develop new skills.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you are concerned about your baby's milk intake, weight gain, or eating habits, talk to your pediatrician. They can provide personalized guidance and support.
