When Should You Worry About a 1-Year-Old Starting Solid Foods?

By the time your baby turns 1, they should already be eating a variety of solid foods. However, if your child has been slow to start solids or is a picky eater, you may wonder when to worry. This guide covers the normal timeline for starting solids and the red flags to watch for.

When Is Starting Solids Normal?

The American Academy of Pediatrics and other health organizations recommend starting solid foods around 6 months of age (Source: NIH | Infant and Newborn Nutrition: MedlinePlus – Infant and Newborn Nutrition). At this age, most babies are developmentally ready to begin exploring foods. By 12 months, a child should be eating a variety of solid foods as a primary source of nutrition, alongside breast milk or formula.
