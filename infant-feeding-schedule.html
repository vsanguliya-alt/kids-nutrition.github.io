Infant Feeding Schedule: A Guide for Parents

Establishing a consistent feeding schedule is an important part of caring for your baby. While the exact schedule will vary based on your baby&#x27;s age and needs, understanding the basics can help you create a routine that works for your family.

How Often Should You Feed Your Baby?

For a newborn, feeding on demand is recommended. This means feeding your baby whenever they show signs of hunger, such as rooting, lip-smacking, or crying. In the first few weeks, this will be about every 1 to 3 hours. Breast milk contains the right balance of nutrients to help your infant grow into a strong and healthy toddler (Source: NIH).

As your baby grows, the time between feedings will lengthen. By 3 to 4 months, many babies can go 3 to 4 hours between feeds. By 6 months, as solid foods are introduced, the number of milk feedings will gradually decrease.

Sample Feeding Schedule by Age

0 to 3 Months

Feeding on demand. This will be roughly every 1 to 3 hours.

Breast milk or formula only. No other foods or liquids.

4 to 6 Months

Feedings every 3 to 4 hours.

Breast milk or formula: About 24 to 32 ounces per day.

Start solids: At about 6 months of age, you can start introducing single-ingredient foods.

6 to 9 Months

Feedings every 3 to 4 hours.

Breast milk or formula: About 24 to 32 ounces per day.

Solids: 2 to 3 meals per day, plus snacks.

9 to 12 Months

Feedings every 4 hours.

Breast milk or formula: About 20 to 28 ounces per day.

Solids: 3 meals per day, plus 2 snacks.

What to Include in Feedings

Breast milk or formula: The primary source of nutrition for the first year.

Solid foods: When you start solids, introduce one new food at a time.

Water: Offer water in a cup with meals once solids are started.

Avoid: Do not give your baby honey before 1 year of age. Babies should not drink juice before age 1.

When to See a Doctor

If you have concerns about your baby&#x27;s feeding, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
