Newborn Hydration: A Parent's Guide

Keeping your newborn well-hydrated is essential for their health. For newborns, their primary source of hydration is breast milk or formula. Breast milk contains the right balance of nutrients to help your infant grow into a strong and healthy toddler. Some of the nutrients in breast milk also help protect your infant against some common childhood illnesses and infections.

Primary Source of Hydration

For newborns, breast milk or formula provides all the hydration they need, even in hot weather. In the first few days, your baby will receive colostrum, which is rich in nutrients and antibodies. Your baby may need to feed more frequently in warm weather, so follow their cues and offer milk more often.

How to Offer Fluids

For a newborn, fluids are offered through frequent feedings. In the first few weeks, this will be about every 1 to 3 hours. You should feed your baby on demand, watching for signs of hunger such as rooting, lip-smacking, or crying.

Signs of Dehydration

Newborns are especially prone to dehydration because their bodies are less efficient at conserving water. Watch for these signs of dehydration:

Fewer wet diapers: Fewer than 6 wet diapers in 24 hours.

Dark urine: The urine may appear dark yellow or orange.

Dry mouth and lips: A dry tongue and mouth.

No tears: Crying without tears.

Sunken eyes or soft spot: A sunken "soft spot" on the head.

Excessive sleepiness: Lethargy or fussiness.

When to See a Doctor

Call your baby's doctor right away if your baby shows any signs of dehydration. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
