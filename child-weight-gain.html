Healthy Weight Gain for Your Child: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

As a parent, one of the most common concerns is whether your child is growing as they should. Understanding what healthy weight gain looks like and how to support it can help ease your worries.

What Is a Healthy Weight for a Child?

There is no single &quot;healthy&quot; weight for a child. Every child is different, and growth is determined by a combination of genetics, nutrition, and activity level. The best way to assess your child&#x27;s growth is by looking at their growth curve over time, not by comparing them to other children.

Your pediatrician will track your child&#x27;s weight and height at routine well-child visits. These visits are important to your child&#x27;s health. As long as your child is following their own growth curve, they are likely growing at a healthy rate.

How to Support Healthy Weight Gain

Focus on Nutrient-Dense Foods

Children have small stomachs, so it&#x27;s important to make every bite count. Offer a variety of nutrient-dense foods that are high in healthy fats and calories.

Healthy sources of calories and nutrients include:

Healthy fats: Avocado, olive oil, full-fat yogurt, cheese, and nut butters.

Protein: Eggs, fish, poultry, and beans.

Complex carbohydrates: Whole grains, sweet potatoes, and fruit.

The Importance of Portion Sizes

A child&#x27;s portion size is much smaller than an adult&#x27;s. A general rule of thumb is that a serving size should be about the size of their fist.

Avoid Empty Calories

Limit sugary drinks, sweets, and highly processed foods. These offer empty calories with little nutritional value and can interfere with your child&#x27;s appetite for more nutrient-dense foods.

Create a Healthy Plate

A healthy diet helps children grow and learn. To give your child a nutritious diet:

Make half of what is on your child&#x27;s plate fruits and vegetables (Source: NIH).

Choose healthy sources of protein, such as lean meat, nuts, and eggs (Source: NIH).

Serve whole-grain breads and cereals because they are high in fiber (Source: NIH).

When to Be Concerned

If you are worried about your child&#x27;s weight gain, speak with your pediatrician. Signs to watch for include:

Weight loss or not gaining weight appropriately.

Poor appetite that doesn&#x27;t improve over time.

Changes in behavior, such as seeming overly tired or irritable.

Delayed developmental milestones.

Trust Your Instincts

If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider right away. Well-child visits are an essential part of tracking your child&#x27;s development and addressing any concerns you may have.
