Vitamin D for Your 18-Month-Old: A Parent's Guide

Vitamin D is an essential nutrient that plays a vital role in your child's health. Why Vitamin D Is Important

Vitamin D is crucial for your child's body to absorb calcium, which is essential for building strong, healthy bones.

Prevents Rickets: In young children, a severe and prolonged lack of vitamin D can lead to rickets, a condition that causes soft, weak bones and can lead to bone deformities, delayed growth, and dental problems (Source: NIH).

Supports a Healthy Immune System: Adequate vitamin D levels also play a key role in the immune system.

Where Do 18-Month-Olds Get Vitamin D?

While the body can produce vitamin D when the skin is exposed to sunlight, it can be hard to get enough from sun exposure alone. For this reason, diet is a very important source.

Food Sources: Vitamin D is naturally present in very few foods, but some are fortified with it. Good sources include fatty fish like salmon, egg yolks, and fortified foods like some cow's milk, yogurt, and cereals.

Supplements: Most pediatricians recommend a daily vitamin D supplement for children, especially those who are breastfed or do not drink enough fortified milk. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends a daily supplement of 400 IU for all infants and children who do not get enough from diet.

How Much Vitamin D Does Your Child Need?

The sources do not specify a dose for an 18-month-old. You should always consult your pediatrician for the most up-to-date and personalized recommendation for your child's vitamin D needs. The recommended amount can vary based on factors like their diet, where you live, and their overall health.

When to See a Doctor

If you are concerned that your child might not be getting enough vitamin D, or if you have any questions about their nutrition and growth, speak with your pediatrician. They can provide guidance and, if necessary, recommend a suitable supplement and dose.
