4-Year-Old Feeding Schedule: A Guide for Parents

At 4 years old, your child is becoming more independent and their feeding schedule is an important part of their daily routine. A consistent schedule can help ensure they are getting the nutrition they need and can also help prevent power struggles at mealtimes.

How Much Should a 4-Year-Old Eat?

A 4-year-old needs about 1,200 to 1,800 calories per day, distributed across three meals and two snacks. A preschooler&#x27;s serving size is generally about one-quarter to one-third of an adult portion.

Sample Feeding Schedule

A structured routine can provide a sense of security and help prevent power struggles. Here is a sample schedule you can adapt:

Time Activity

7:00 AM Wake up

8:00 AM Breakfast

10:00 AM Morning Snack

12:00 PM Lunch

3:00 PM Afternoon Snack

5:30 PM Dinner

7:00 PM Bedtime routine begins

What to Include in Meals and Snacks

Preschoolers need a variety of nutrients each day, including:

Protein: Eggs, poultry, fish, beans, and yogurt.

Healthy fats: Avocado, nut butters, and full-fat dairy.

Complex carbohydrates: Whole grains, fruits, and starchy vegetables.

Vitamins and minerals: Offer a colorful variety of fruits and vegetables.

Common Feeding Challenges

Picky Eating: At 4 years old, picky eating is very common. It&#x27;s a normal part of a child&#x27;s development as they assert their independence. Offer a variety of healthy foods, and do not force your child to eat.

Refusing Food: Avoid power struggles. Keep offering a variety of healthy foods without pressuring your child to eat.

Eating Too Much or Too Little: Trust your child to eat what they need.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s eating habits, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. Well-child visits are important to your child&#x27;s health. If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider.
