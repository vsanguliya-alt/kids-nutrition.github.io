2-Year-Old Feeding Schedule: A Guide for Parents

At 2 years old, your child is becoming more independent, and their feeding schedule is an important part of their daily routine. A consistent schedule can help ensure they are getting the nutrition they need and can also help prevent power struggles at mealtimes.

How Much Should a 2-Year-Old Eat?

A 2-year-old needs about 1,000 to 1,400 calories per day, distributed across three meals and two snacks. A toddler's serving size is generally about one-quarter of an adult portion.

Sample Feeding Schedule

A structured routine can provide a sense of security and help prevent power struggles. Here is a sample schedule you can adapt:

Time Activity

7:00 AM Wake up

8:00 AM Breakfast

10:00 AM Morning Snack

12:00 PM Lunch

3:00 PM Afternoon Snack

5:30 PM Dinner

7:00 PM Bedtime routine begins

What to Include in Meals and Snacks

Toddlers need a variety of nutrients each day, including:

Protein: Eggs, poultry, fish, beans, and yogurt.

Healthy fats: Avocado, nut butters, and full-fat dairy.

Complex carbohydrates: Whole grains, fruits, and starchy vegetables.

Vitamins and minerals: Offer a colorful variety of fruits and vegetables.

Common Feeding Challenges

Picky Eating: At 2 years old, picky eating is very common. It's a normal part of a toddler's development as they assert their independence. Offer a variety of healthy foods, and do not force your child to eat.

Refusing Food: Avoid power struggles. Keep offering a variety of healthy foods without pressuring your child to eat.

Eating Too Much or Too Little: Trust your child to eat what they need.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child's eating habits, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. Well-child visits are important to your toddler's health. If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider.
