Starting Solid Foods: A Guide for Parents in New York

Introducing solid foods is an exciting milestone. For parents in New York, understanding when and how to start, as well as local health considerations, can help you navigate this stage with confidence.

When to Start Solid Foods

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommend starting solid foods at about 6 months of age . This aligns with guidelines from the World Health Organization (WHO) . Before 4 months, solid food is not needed nutritionally and can be difficult for babies to manage due to the extrusion reflex . Introducing solid foods before 4 months may also be associated with an increased risk of food allergies .

Your baby is likely ready for solid foods when they can:

Sit up alone or with support .

Control their head and neck .

Open their mouth when food is offered .

Swallow food rather than pushing it back out .

What Foods to Offer First

The order of introducing foods is not critical, but offering a wide variety of tastes and textures early on is beneficial . The AAP recommends including foods that provide essential nutrients like iron and zinc, such as pureed meats and iron-fortified cereals .

Good first foods include:

Iron-fortified infant cereal (oat, barley, or multigrain) .

Pureed meats (beef, chicken, turkey) .

Pureed vegetables (peas, sweet potatoes) .

Pureed fruits (bananas, pears) .

Yogurt without added sugars .

Foods to avoid in the first year:

Honey (risk of infant botulism) .

Cow's milk as a drink (does not have the right nutrients and can be hard to digest) .

Choking hazards such as whole nuts, hot dogs, grapes, popcorn, and hard candy .

Introducing Peanuts and Other Allergens

Current guidelines recommend introducing peanut-containing foods to most babies at about 6 months of age to help prevent peanut allergies . For babies with severe eczema or an egg allergy, talk to your pediatrician about the best way to introduce peanuts, as testing may be recommended . The same approach applies to other common allergens like eggs and fish .

Solid Foods and Lead Poisoning Prevention in New York

In New York, lead poisoning is a significant health concern, particularly for young children living in older homes with lead-based paint . A healthy diet can help protect children from the effects of lead exposure . Feeding your child foods rich in calcium, iron, and vitamin C can reduce lead absorption .

Foods high in calcium: milk, cheese, yogurt .

Foods high in iron: beans, meat, spinach, eggs, fortified cereal .

Foods high in vitamin C: oranges, grapefruits, tomatoes, green peppers .

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor about starting solids?

Call your pediatrician if you have concerns about your baby's readiness for solids, if they are showing signs of poor growth, or if they have difficulty swallowing, gagging, or vomiting after eating .

How long does the transition to solid foods take?

It's a gradual process. It can take a few weeks to a few months for your baby to eat a variety of foods and textures. Be patient and consistent .

What home remedies help with feeding difficulties?

If your baby struggles with a new texture, try offering it in a different form (e.g., smooth puree to lumpy). Offer new foods alongside familiar favorites, and avoid pressuring your baby to eat. It can take many exposures for a baby to accept a new food .

Is it normal for a 6-month-old to refuse solids?

Yes, it's common for babies to refuse new foods at first. However, if they are consistently refusing all solids and not gaining weight, talk to your pediatrician.

Does teething affect my baby's eating habits?

Teething can cause discomfort and lead to temporary changes in appetite. If your baby is otherwise healthy and continues to gain weight, it is usually not a cause for worry.

When should I go to the ER for a feeding problem?

Go to the ER immediately if your baby has severe difficulty breathing, is choking, or has a severe allergic reaction (hives, swelling, difficulty breathing). These are emergency signs.
