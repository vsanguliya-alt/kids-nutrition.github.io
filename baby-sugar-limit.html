Sugar Limit for Your Baby: A Parent's Guide

The first years of life are a critical time for establishing healthy eating habits. For a baby, it's especially important to limit added sugar.

The Recommendation

The American Heart Association (AHA) and other health organizations recommend no added sugars for children under 2 years of age. This means that babies should not be given foods or drinks with any sugar added during processing or preparation. Because it contains a lot of sugar, babies should not drink juice before age 1 (Source: NIH).

Why Sugar Should Be Avoided

Nutritional Displacement: A baby's stomach is small, and it's important to fill it with nutrient-dense foods that provide vitamins, minerals, and energy for growth. Added sugar offers empty calories and can displace healthier foods like fruits, vegetables, and proteins.

Developing Taste Preferences: Introducing sweet foods early can shape a child's long-term taste preferences, making them more likely to prefer sweet foods later in life.

Dental Health: Even before teeth erupt, sugar in the mouth can promote the growth of bacteria that cause tooth decay.

Health Risks: Early and excessive sugar intake is linked to an increased risk of childhood obesity, type 2 diabetes, and other health issues.

Sources of Added Sugar to Watch Out For

Sweetened beverages (juice, soda, fruit drinks)

Infant desserts and puddings

Sweetened yogurts

Processed snacks and cereals

Baby foods with added fruit purees or syrups

What to Offer Instead

Fruit: Offer naturally sweet fruits like mashed banana or pureed applesauce without added sugar.

Vegetables: Introduce a variety of vegetables, which help build a palate for less sweet flavors.

Water: Once your baby starts solids, you can offer a few sips of water with meals. Offer water or milk instead of sugary drinks.

When to See a Doctor

If you have any concerns about your baby's diet or growth, talk to your pediatrician. They can offer personalized guidance and support.
