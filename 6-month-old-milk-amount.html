How Much Milk Does a 6-Month-Old Need? A Guide for Parents

At 6 months old, breast milk or formula remains your baby's primary source of nutrition. Understanding how much they need and how to manage common issues related to milk feeding is important for their healthy growth.

The Recommended Daily Amount

A 6-month-old typically consumes about 24 to 32 ounces (approximately 720 to 950 milliliters) of breast milk or formula over a 24-hour period. This is usually divided into 4 to 6 feedings . While this is a general guide, every baby is different. Your baby's hunger cues are the best indicator of whether they are getting enough.

Feeding as a Main Source of Nutrition

Until your baby is at least 12 months old, breast milk or formula should be their main drink . These provide the complete balance of fats, proteins, vitamins, and minerals a growing baby needs. Solid foods introduced at 6 months are for practice and supplemental nutrition, not to replace milk feeds.

Recognizing Signs of Overfeeding

One key to successful feeding is to avoid overfeeding your baby . Giving them more milk than they need can lead to issues like reflux and spitting up. Following their hunger and fullness cues—such as turning away or stopping sucking—can help you avoid overfeeding. If you are using formula, stick to the amount recommended by your pediatrician or as guided by your baby's cues.

When to Contact a Doctor

Contact your child's health care provider if you have concerns about feeding, such as:

Your baby is consistently spitting up large amounts after most feeds.

Your baby is not gaining weight appropriately.

Your baby shows signs of a sensitivity to milk protein, in which case your provider might suggest switching formula .
