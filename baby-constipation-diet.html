Baby Constipation Diet: 7 Signs Parents Should Know

Constipation in babies can be distressing for both the child and the parents. While it is often a temporary issue related to diet, there are clear signs that indicate a problem may be more serious. Here are 7 key signs that parents should know about baby constipation and when dietary changes are needed.

1. Hard, Dry, or Pellet-Like Stools

This is the most definitive sign of constipation. If your baby's poo looks like firm, dry pellets that do not soak into the nappy, they are constipated . For older children, a helpful comparison is that stools should look like soft-serve ice cream; they should not be hard, dry, or large enough to clog the toilet .

2. Pain or Difficulty Passing Stools

If your baby is straining, crying, or seems to be in pain during a bowel movement, this is a key sign . While babies may grunt or turn red when passing a soft stool as they learn to use their muscles, significant pain or distress is a red flag .

3. Pooing Less Frequently Than Usual

A change in your baby's normal pattern is a warning sign. While breastfed babies can go up to a week without a bowel movement , a general indicator of constipation is having fewer than 3 poos in a week . If your baby is passing stool less often than their usual routine, it's time to monitor the consistency.

4. A Firm or Swollen Tummy

A tummy that feels hard or looks distended can be a sign that stool is backed up in the intestines . This is often accompanied by discomfort and gassiness.

5. Loss of Appetite or Irritability

Constipation can make babies feel uncomfortable and full. This can lead to them being less hungry than usual, lacking energy, and being irritable or grumpy .

6. Blood in the Stool

Streaks of bright red blood on the surface of a hard stool can indicate a small tear (anal fissure) in the anus from straining . Blood in the stool should always prompt a visit with your pediatrician .

7. Persistent Constipation Despite Diet Changes

If you have increased fluids and offered fibre-rich foods (such as pureed prunes, pears, or peas) and your baby remains constipated for several days, it is time to consult a doctor . If a baby cannot pass a bowel movement after 4 days, it's a sign to seek medical advice .

What to Do

If your baby shows signs of constipation, dietary changes are the first step. For bottle-fed babies, you can offer cooled boiled water between feeds . For babies on solids, increase high-fibre foods like pureed prunes, pears, peaches, beans, and peas . Gentle tummy massage and bicycling the legs can also help .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my baby's constipation?

Call your pediatrician if your baby has blood in the stool, a swollen belly, vomiting, a fever, is not eating and seems unusually tired, or if constipation does not improve after a few days of home treatment .

Q: How long does constipation normally last?

With dietary changes and home care, constipation usually improves within a few days. It may take a few days to get things moving again . If it persists for more than a week, speak to your health visitor or GP .

Q: What home remedies help with baby constipation?

Offer extra water between feeds (if bottle-fed), add fibre-rich foods like pureed prunes, pears, and peas to their diet, and gently move their legs in a "bicycling" motion . A gentle tummy massage can also help .

Q: Does formula cause constipation in babies?

Yes, it's quite common for babies to become constipated when they start formula, which is harder to digest than breast milk . Always follow the formula instructions; adding too much powder can cause constipation and dehydration.

Q: Can teething affect my baby's eating habits and cause constipation?

Teething can cause discomfort and lead to changes in eating habits, which may reduce fluid intake and potentially contribute to constipation . Ensure your baby is getting enough fluids.

Q: When should I go to the ER for my baby's constipation?

Go to the ER immediately if your baby has constipation with a swollen belly, forceful vomiting, fever, or if they are very difficult to wake . Also, if a newborn does not pass meconium in the first 48 hours of life .
