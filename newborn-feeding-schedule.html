Newborn Feeding Schedule: A Guide for Parents

Establishing a consistent feeding schedule is an important part of caring for your newborn. For a newborn, feeding on demand is recommended. This means feeding your baby whenever they show signs of hunger, such as rooting, lip-smacking, or crying. In the first few weeks, this will be about every 1 to 3 hours.

How Often Should You Feed Your Newborn?

Newborns have tiny stomachs and need to feed frequently. In the first month, you can expect your baby to feed 8 to 12 times a day. As your baby grows, the time between feedings will lengthen.

How Much Should Your Newborn Eat?

Breastfed babies: You can't measure exactly how much a breastfed baby eats. Instead, watch for signs that your baby is getting enough: at least 6 wet diapers a day, steady weight gain, and contentment after feedings.

Formula-fed babies: In the first few days, a newborn may only take about 1 to 2 ounces (30 to 60 ml) per feeding. By the end of the first month, this may increase to 3 to 4 ounces (90 to 120 ml) per feeding.

Feeding Cues to Watch For

Your baby will give you clear signs when they are hungry. Try to feed your baby when they show early signs of hunger:

Rooting: Turning their head toward your hand or breast.

Lip-smacking: Making sucking motions with their lips.

Bringing hands to mouth.

Crying: Crying is a late sign of hunger. It can be harder to settle a crying baby to feed.

Important Feeding Safety Tips

Do not give your baby honey before 1 year of age.

Babies should not drink juice before age 1.

Avoid cow's milk before age 1, since it does not have all of the nutrients.

Always hold your baby during feedings. Do not prop the bottle.

When to See a Doctor

If you have concerns about your baby's feeding, weight gain, or growth, talk to your pediatrician. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
