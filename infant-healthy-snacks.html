Healthy Snacks for Your Infant: A Guide for Parents

Food provides the energy and nutrients that young children need to be healthy (Source: NIH). Offering healthy snacks is an excellent way to ensure your baby gets the nutrition they need to grow, learn, and stay healthy.

When to Start Snacks

You can start offering small snacks once your baby is eating solid foods, typically around 6 months of age. Snacks should be small, easy to eat, and nutritious.

Healthy Snack Ideas

Here are some healthy, baby-friendly snack ideas:

Soft fruits: Mashed or very soft pieces of banana, avocado, pear, or peach.

Cooked vegetables: Soft, cooked vegetable sticks or mashed vegetables like sweet potato, carrot, or peas.

Yogurt: Plain, unsweetened whole milk yogurt is a great source of calcium and protein.

Cheese: Soft cheese strips or small cubes of mild cheese.

Crackers: Whole grain teething biscuits or crackers.

Toast: Strips of whole wheat toast.

Tips for Healthy Snacking

Limit sweet snacks and drinks.

Avoid putting the baby to bed with a bottle.

Check teeth regularly for spots or stains.

Snacks to Avoid

Honey: Do not give your baby honey before 1 year of age.

Cow&#x27;s milk: Avoid cow&#x27;s milk before age 1.

Juice: Babies should not drink juice before age 1.

Choking hazards: Avoid hard, round foods like whole grapes or nuts. Always supervise your baby during eating.

When to See a Doctor

If you have any concerns about your baby&#x27;s diet or growth, talk to your pediatrician.
