Infant Hydration: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Keeping your baby well-hydrated is essential for their health. Water helps regulate body temperature, aids digestion, and keeps cells functioning properly. For infants, their primary source of hydration is breast milk or formula.

Primary Source of Hydration

For babies under 6 months, breast milk or formula provides all the hydration they need, even in hot weather. Your baby may need to feed more frequently in warm weather, so follow their cues and offer milk more often.

When to Introduce Water

You can start offering small amounts of water once your baby starts eating solid foods, usually around 6 months of age. Offer a few sips of water from a cup with meals. This helps your baby learn to drink from a cup and can be part of their routine. The recommended amount is about 4 to 8 ounces (120 to 240 ml) of water per day in addition to breast milk or formula. This is just a small amount and should not replace milk feeds.

How to Offer Water

Use a cup: Introduce a sippy cup or an open cup for water.

Offer with meals: Offer water at mealtimes.

Don&#x27;t overdo it: Don&#x27;t replace milk feeds with water.

Signs of Dehydration

Babies and young children are especially prone to dehydration because their bodies are less efficient at conserving water. Watch for these signs of dehydration:

Fewer wet diapers: For infants, fewer than 6 wet diapers in 24 hours.

Dry mouth and lips: A dry tongue and mouth.

No tears: Crying without tears.

Sunken eyes or soft spot: A sunken &quot;soft spot&quot; on the head.

Excessive sleepiness: Lethargy or fussiness.

When to See a Doctor

Call your baby&#x27;s doctor right away if your baby shows any signs of dehydration. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider.
