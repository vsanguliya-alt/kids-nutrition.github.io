Baby Picky Eating: What Parents Need to Know

It&#x27;s common for babies to become fussy about food, especially around 9 to 18 months of age. This is often a normal developmental phase, not a sign of a problem. However, it can be stressful for parents who worry about their baby&#x27;s growth and nutrition.

Why Is My Baby a Picky Eater?

There are several reasons why a baby might become picky:

Developmental Changes: As babies grow, they become more mobile and interested in their environment, which can be a distraction during mealtimes.

Teething: Teething can make chewing and swallowing uncomfortable.

New Skills: Babies may be practicing new skills like the pincer grasp and may prefer to explore food with their fingers rather than eat it.

Decreased Appetite: A growth spurt often causes a temporary increase in appetite, followed by a decrease.

Reflux or GERD: Gastroesophageal reflux (GER) can cause feeding problems, discomfort, or other symptoms that affect your baby&#x27;s growth or sleep. More serious cases (GERD) may cause arching of the back, gagging, poor eating, and not gaining enough weight (Source: NIH).

Strategies for Handling Picky Eating

Here are some evidence-based strategies to navigate this phase:

1. Keep Offering a Variety of Foods

Continue to offer a wide range of healthy foods, even if your baby has rejected them before. It can take up to 10 to 15 exposures for a baby to accept a new food. Don&#x27;t force the issue, but keep offering.

2. Make Mealtimes Positive

Avoid pressure and power struggles. Let your baby explore food at their own pace. It&#x27;s okay if they don&#x27;t eat much; mealtimes are about learning and socializing too.

3. Offer Finger Foods

Babies often enjoy self-feeding. Offer soft, easily dissolvable finger foods like cooked vegetable sticks, toast strips, or cheese cubes.

4. Involve Your Baby

Let your baby touch and play with food, even if it gets messy. This sensory exploration is an important part of learning to eat.

5. Stick to a Routine

A regular meal and snack schedule can help your baby feel secure and regulate their appetite.

When to See a Doctor

While picky eating is usually normal, you should consult your pediatrician if your baby:

Is not gaining weight or is losing weight.

Is refusing all foods and drinks.

Has signs of a medical issue, such as persistent vomiting, diarrhea, or severe constipation.

Is showing signs of a developmental delay.

Has symptoms of GERD, such as arching of the back, gagging, poor eating, and not gaining enough weight (Source: NIH).

If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away.
