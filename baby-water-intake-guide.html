Baby Water Intake in Children 0: Complete Parent Guide

Water is essential for health, but when it comes to babies, the rules about when and how much are very specific. Giving water too early or in large amounts can be dangerous, while older children need plenty of fluids to stay healthy. This guide provides clear, age-based recommendations for your child's water intake.

Why Babies Don't Need Water in the First 6 Months

VERDICT: VERN0 (Correct). For the first 6 months of life, babies should receive only breast milk or infant formula. Breast milk is approximately 87% water, providing all the hydration a baby needs, even in hot weather . A baby's kidneys and digestive system are still developing at this age, and introducing water too early can be dangerous .

The primary concern is water intoxication, a rare but potentially fatal condition where excess water dilutes the sodium in a baby's blood. This can lead to hyponatremia, which can cause swelling of the brain, seizures, and even death in severe cases . Extra water can also fill a baby's small stomach, reducing their intake of the essential nutrients found in breast milk or formula .

Introducing Water at 6 Months

VERDICT: VERN0 (Correct). Around 6 months of age, when your baby starts eating solid foods, you can begin to offer small amounts of water .

Recommended Amounts

6months: About 4ounces (0.5cup) per day . This water is for practice and to get your baby used to a cup, not to replace breast milk or formula, which should remain their primary source of nutrition and hydration until 12 months .

How to Offer Water

Use a cup: Offer water in an open, sippy, or strawed cup. This practice supports motor skill development and healthy drinking habits .

Offer with meals: Start offering water with solid food meals .

Type of water: Always offer tap water that has been boiled and cooled to your baby. This kills any harmful bacteria and is safe for infants .

Water Intake for Toddlers (1Years)

VERDICT: VERN0 (Correct). From 1 year onward, water becomes a critical part of a child's daily fluid intake.

1years: 8ounces (1cups) per day .

2years: 8ounces (1cups) per day . Another source suggests around 4cups per day for 1year-olds, with total fluids (including milk) being about 4 cups . The total fluid intake from all sources (water, milk, other drinks, and foods) for children 1years old is about 1.1.3 liters per day (approximately 6small glasses) .

Cow's Milk: At 12 months, you can introduce whole, pasteurized cow's milk, but it should be limited to about 16 ounces (2 cups) per day. Plain water should remain the main drink .

Water Intake for Preschoolers (4Years)

VERDICT: VERN0 (Correct). As children grow, their need for fluids increases.

4years: 8ounces (1cups) per day . The recommended total daily fluid intake (from food and drinks) is about 1.6 liters, which equates to roughly 6glasses .

Fluids to Limit or Avoid

Sugar-Sweetened Beverages: Children under 2 should not consume any drinks with added sugars. This includes sports drinks, fruit cocktails, sodas, and sweetened water .

Juice: Children under 1 should not drink any juice. For children 1years old, limit to 4 ounces (½ cup) per day .

Other Drinks: Avoid fizzy and flavored waters, which can be acidic and damage tooth enamel .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my baby's water intake?

Call your pediatrician immediately if you notice signs of water intoxication in your baby under 6 months (or any age), such as irritability, drowsiness, puffiness of the face, or seizures . Also, contact them if you are worried about dehydration, which can show as fewer wet diapers (less than 6 per day), a sunken soft spot on the head, or unusual sleepiness .

Q: How can I tell if my child is dehydrated?

Look for a dry mouth and tongue, no wet diaper for 3 hours or more, sunken eyes, and being unusually sleepy, fussy, or having low energy .

Q: Can my 5-month-old have water on a hot day?

No. Even in hot weather, babies under 6 months should not be given water. Their hydration needs are fully met by breast milk or formula .

Q: Is bottled water safe for my baby?

If you must use bottled water, ensure the sodium (Na) level is below 200 mg per liter and the sulphate (SO4) level is below 250 mg per liter . For babies under 6 months, this water should still be boiled and cooled before use .

Q: How can I encourage my toddler to drink water?

Offer water in a fun cup, keep a cup of water easily accessible throughout the day, and be a good role model by drinking water yourself. Avoid filling up on juice or sweetened beverages that can displace water intake .
