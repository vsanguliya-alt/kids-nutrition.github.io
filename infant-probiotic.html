Probiotics for Your Infant: What the Science Says

Probiotics are often marketed as a way to support digestive health, but when it comes to infants, the evidence is limited and the recommendations are cautious.

What the Evidence Shows

The sources provided do not contain robust evidence to support the use of probiotics in infants for general health or specific conditions. The only mention of probiotics in the context of infant health relates to colic.

No evidence for colic: Probiotic supplements are not recommended for colic and there is no evidence they help with this condition in infants (Source: NHS).

Bowel Movements and Digestive Health

While probiotics are sometimes used to address digestive issues like diarrhea or constipation, the sources note that these conditions can have various causes. If your infant is experiencing persistent digestive issues, it is important to seek professional medical advice to determine the underlying cause rather than relying on supplements.

When to See a Doctor

If your infant is experiencing digestive issues — whether constipation, diarrhea, or general feeding problems — it's important to seek professional medical advice rather than trying over-the-counter supplements. A pediatrician can:

Evaluate your infant's symptoms and identify underlying causes.

Recommend evidence-based dietary changes.

Advise on any appropriate treatments, which may include probiotics in specific cases, but only if supported by evidence and tailored to your infant's needs.

If you are worried about your infant, call your health care provider right away.
