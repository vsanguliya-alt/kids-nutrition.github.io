Infant Constipation Relief: A Focus on Diet

Constipation in infants can be uncomfortable and distressing for both you and your baby. The good news is that for most babies, constipation can be effectively managed with simple changes to their diet. This guide provides evidence-based dietary strategies to help relieve and prevent constipation in your baby.

Understanding Baby Constipation

Constipation means having fewer than three bowel movements a week (Source: NIH). However, it&#x27;s important to remember that bowel habits vary from baby to baby. It&#x27;s not important that you have a bowel movement every day (Source: NIH). Instead, look for signs like dry, hard, or pellet-like poo, straining or pain when passing stool, and a swollen or painful tummy.

Dietary Changes for Constipation

If your baby is over 6 months old and has started solids, dietary changes are usually the first line of treatment.

Increase Fiber Intake

One of the most effective ways to prevent constipation is to increase your baby&#x27;s intake of high-fiber foods (Source: NIH). Good options include:

Fruits: Prunes, pears, peaches, plums, and apples (with skin). You can offer these as purees or soft, cooked pieces.

Vegetables: Broccoli, peas, sweet potatoes, and carrots (cooked until soft).

Legumes: Cooked and mashed beans or lentils.

The &quot;P&quot; Fruits

Fruits that start with the letter &quot;P&quot; are particularly effective for relieving constipation. These include prunes, peaches, pears, and plums. They contain sorbitol, a natural sugar alcohol that draws water into the intestines, helping to soften the stool.

Ensure Adequate Hydration

Fiber needs water to work effectively. Increasing your baby&#x27;s fluid intake is crucial to prevent dehydration and help soften the stool. Offer water in a cup with meals.

What to Avoid

Limit binding foods: Foods like bananas, applesauce, and rice cereal can be binding for some babies.

Avoid sugary drinks: Drinks high in sugar can displace water and contribute to constipation.

Avoid cow&#x27;s milk before age 1: It doesn&#x27;t have all of the nutrients.

When to See a Doctor

Constipation in babies can usually be treated at home. However, you should contact your baby&#x27;s healthcare provider if:

Your baby is under 6 months old and has not passed a stool for more than 24 hours.

Your baby has severe abdominal pain, vomiting, or weight loss.

There is blood in the stool.

If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away.
