Iron-Rich Foods for Your Newborn: A Complete Guide

Newborns are born with iron stores that last about six months. During the first 4 to 6 months of life, your baby gets the iron they need from breast milk or iron-fortified formula.

How Newborns Get Iron

Breastfed Babies: Breast milk contains some iron, but it&#x27;s not enough to meet all of a baby&#x27;s needs after 6 months. However, the iron in breast milk is very well absorbed.

Formula-Fed Babies: Most infant formulas are fortified with iron. Your baby will get the iron they need from formula.

When to Start Iron-Rich Foods

At about 6 months of age, your baby&#x27;s iron stores begin to deplete. This is the time to start introducing iron-rich solid foods. Infants are usually ready to eat solid foods at about 6 months of age (Source: NIH).

Best Iron-Rich Foods for Babies

Iron in food comes in two forms: heme and non-heme. Heme iron, found in animal products, is more easily absorbed by the body than non-heme iron from plants (Source: NIH).

Heme Iron Sources (Animal-Based)

Lean meat, seafood, and poultry (Source: NIH).

Eggs: A great source of iron and protein.

Fish: Salmon, trout, and tuna.

Non-Heme Iron Sources (Plant-Based)

Iron-fortified breakfast cereals and breads (Source: NIH).

White beans, lentils, spinach, kidney beans, and peas (Source: NIH).

Nuts and some dried fruits, such as raisins (Source: NIH).

How to Boost Iron Absorption

The body doesn&#x27;t absorb non-heme iron as easily, but you can help by pairing it with vitamin C. This significantly increases how much iron your baby takes in.

Great vitamin C pairings include:

Fruits: Oranges, strawberries, kiwi, and mango.

Vegetables: Broccoli, bell peppers, and tomatoes.

What to Avoid

Cow&#x27;s milk before 12 months: It contains very little iron and can actually make it harder for the body to absorb iron from other foods. It also fills your baby&#x27;s small stomach, reducing their appetite for iron-rich foods.

Honey before 12 months: This is a safety risk due to infant botulism, not iron absorption.

When to See a Doctor

If you are concerned that your baby might not be getting enough iron, or if you have any questions about their nutrition and growth, speak with your pediatrician. They can provide guidance and, if necessary, recommend a suitable supplement and dose.
