Protein Needs for Your Child: A Parent's Guide

Protein is an essential nutrient that plays a vital role in your child's growth and development. Ensuring your child gets enough protein supports everything from building muscles to maintaining a healthy immune system.

Why Protein Is Important

Proteins are the building blocks of the body. They help to build and repair tissues, produce enzymes and hormones, and support a healthy immune system. For a growing child, adequate protein intake is critical for healthy development.

How Much Protein Does a Child Need?

The amount of protein a child needs changes as they grow.

Children (1years): About 13 grams of protein per day.

Children (4years): About 19 grams of protein per day.

Here is what 13 grams of protein looks like in everyday foods:

1 egg

½ cup (about 120 ml) of whole milk yogurt

2 ounces (about 60 grams) of chicken, turkey, or fish

½ cup (about 120 ml) of cooked beans or lentils

1 tablespoon of peanut butter

Best Sources of Protein for Your Child

Children benefit from a variety of protein sources. These include both animal and plant-based options.

Animal sources: Eggs, poultry, fish, yogurt, and cheese.

Plant-based sources: Cooked beans, lentils, and tofu.

Signs Your Child Might Not Be Getting Enough Protein

Protein-energy undernutrition happens when your body doesn't get enough protein or calories from food (Source: NIH). Signs to watch for include:

Poor growth or not gaining weight as expected.

Lack of energy or seeming tired more often than usual.

Frequent infections or a weakened immune system.

Tips for Including Protein in Your Child's Diet

Start early: Offer a source of protein at every meal, such as eggs, yogurt, or beans.

Get creative: Kids may be more willing to eat protein if it's presented in fun shapes or mixed with foods they love (like adding mashed beans to pasta sauce).

Let them self-feed: Offer small, soft pieces of protein-rich foods that they can easily pick up, which helps build confidence and motor skills.

When to See a Doctor

If you are worried that your child isn't getting enough protein or isn't growing at a healthy rate, speak with your pediatrician. A healthcare professional can assess your child's growth and provide personalized guidance on their nutritional needs. If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider.
